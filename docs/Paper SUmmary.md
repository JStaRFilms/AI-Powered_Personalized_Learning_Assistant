Authors: Meryem Yilmaz Soylu, Jeo [Detail not found]
Year: [Year not found in text]

*   Investigates how students’ AI literacy and prior exposure to AI technologies influence their perceptions of Socratic Mind, an interactive, AI-based formative assessment tool.
Motivation:
*   As AI tools become increasingly embedded in higher education, understanding how students interact with these systems is essential to supporting effective learning experiences.
Methodology:
*   Validated surveys collected data from 338 undergraduates in Computer Science and Business courses, utilizing partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM).

**"AI tutoring can safely and effectively support students: An exploratory RCT in UK classrooms"**
Authors: LearnLM Team, Google & Eedi
Year: 2025

Objective:
*   Evaluates whether a pedagogically fine-tuned generative AI model (LearnLM) can safely and effectively scale access to personalized, one-to-one tutoring.
Motivation:
*   One-to-one tutoring is widely considered the gold standard for personalized education but remains prohibitively expensive and scarce to scale broadly.
Methodology:
*   An exploratory randomized controlled trial (RCT) with 165 secondary school students, integrating LearnLM into chat-based tutoring sessions under the direct supervision of expert human tutors.
Contribution:
*   Demonstrated that AI tutoring support was highly effective; students guided by LearnLM were 5.5 percentage points more likely to solve novel problems compared to those interacting with human tutors alone.
Limitations:
*   The session-by-session randomization isolates immediate outcomes but limits observation of cumulative long-term learning impacts, and the study focuses on structured subjects like math rather than interpretive domains.

**"Intelligent Tutoring Systems In The Age Of LLM-Based Agentic Frameworks - Adapting Small On-Device Language Models For Fact-Checking And Student Compliance Detection"**
Authors: Naderi, Evan Vahid
Year: 2025

Objective:
*   Investigates adapting small, on-device language models for pedagogical agents to fact-check claims and detect student compliance within an Intelligent Tutoring System (ITS).
Motivation:
*   While large cloud-based LLMs present privacy concerns for students, small fine-tuned local models offer a secure and accessible way to deploy advanced reasoning capabilities in education.
Methodology:
*   Fine-tuned small encoder models (ModernBERT) and decoder-only models (Llama 3.2, Phi-4, Qwen 2.5) and evaluated them on a custom benchmark dataset derived from the MetaTutor ITS logs.
Contribution:
*   Demonstrated that small fine-tuned decoder models excel at domain-specific fact-checking, while encoder models are more effective for compliance detection, integrating cognitive architectures with modern LLM capabilities.
Limitations:
*   Studied within a highly specific domain (human circulatory system), limited to only two reasoning actions, and trained heavily on synthetic data produced by larger models.

Year: 2024

*   Proposes and evaluates Tutor CoPilot, a scalable Human-AI collaborative solution to provide expert-like guidance to novice tutors in live teaching contexts.
Motivation:
*   Expert-guided training for K-12 educators is costly and difficult to scale, leaving novice educators struggling to effectively remediate student mistakes in real-time.

*   Collaborated with a chat-based tutoring platform utilizing the "Bridge" adaptation method to instruct LMs to generate effective pedagogical suggestions derived from raw think-aloud data of expert educators.

**"The Psychology of Learning from Machines: Anthropomorphic AI and the Paradox of Automation in Education"**
Authors: Junaid Qadir, Muhammad Mumtaz
Year: 2025

Objective:
*   Synthesizes multiple research traditions to establish a framework for understanding how learners psychologically relate to anthropomorphic AI tutors.
Motivation:
*   AI tutors are entering classrooms at unprecedented speeds, heavily outpacing our grasp of their profound psychological and social consequences.
Methodology:
*   Combined theoretical literature synthesis across human factors and automation psychology with an exploratory naturalistic discourse analysis of over 104,984 YouTube comments on AI-generated educational content.
Contribution:
*   Identified persistent challenges including trust calibration failures, anthropomorphism distraction, and automation ironies; advanced core design principles to support complementary AI-human educational systems.
